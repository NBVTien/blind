# blind-game — Product Context

## Product Purpose

A solo Game Master tool for running "blind map" tabletop sessions. The GM controls everything; players on their own devices see only their own state. The tool is state management with atmosphere — it keeps the GM from losing track while the dungeon stays mysterious.

## Users

**Primary user: the Game Master**
- Running a live session, usually on a laptop or large screen
- Needs quick access to player state, map position, and action controls
- Works under mild performance pressure (players are watching/waiting)
- Values clarity above everything else during play

**Secondary user: the Player**
- On a phone or tablet, often in the same physical space as the GM
- Sees only their own HP/gold/inventory and turn status
- Light UI, minimal cognitive load — they're also paying attention to the GM's narration

## Brand

**Name:** BLIND

**Tone:** Functional theater. The tool is a prop — it should feel like part of the game world, not a SaaS dashboard. Atmospheric, deliberate, unhurried. Never cute. Never corporate.

**Anti-references:** Linear, Notion, Vercel dashboard, anything with a white background and Inter font. No "product" feeling. No gamification chrome (badges, progress bars, confetti).

**Register:** product

## Design Principles

1. **State at a glance.** The GM needs to see player positions, HP, gold, and turn order without scanning. Hierarchy carries the load.
2. **The map is the source of truth.** The SVG map panel is the dominant element in the GM dashboard. Everything else serves it.
3. **Players don't need the map.** The player view is intentionally sparse. Information scarcity is the mechanic.
4. **Atmosphere without decoration.** Dark, warm, old — but never at the expense of readability. Torchlight, not candles.
5. **No card grids.** Lists, dividers, typographic hierarchy. Cards imply equal-weight items; dungeon encounters are not equal.

## Scene Sentences

**GM Dashboard:** A GM managing a live dungeon crawl on a 15-inch laptop, dim room, players huddled around their phones. Needs the full map visible at a glance and player state accessible without tab-switching.

**Player View:** A player checking their phone on the couch, waiting for their turn. Should feel like a character sheet, not an app.

**Map Builder:** GM alone after the session, designing next week's dungeon. Methodical, unhurried. Should feel like drawing on graph paper.

## Color Strategy

**Committed** — amber/ochre carries 40–60% of the interactive surface. Backgrounds are near-void dark with warm chroma. The amber primary is not accent-only; it IS the product color.

## What Success Looks Like

Someone screenshots the GM Dashboard and it looks like it belongs in a tabletop gaming context — not a startup tool. The dark palette and serif fonts signal "dungeon" before the content does.
